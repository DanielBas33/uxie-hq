# UXIE Design System

> Status: initial foundation (v0.1)
> Last updated: 2026-09-27
> Scope: UXIE brand expression across websites, documents, presentations and future digital products.

## 1. Purpose

This document is the canonical starting point for UXIE's visual design system.
It exists to keep UXIE's work recognisable and coherent while the brand is still
being defined.

The system is intentionally incomplete. It establishes the direction already
accepted by UXIE, records provisional hypotheses separately and leaves unresolved
choices open rather than turning them into arbitrary rules.

When creating or reviewing a UXIE visual artifact:

1. follow the confirmed decisions in this document;
2. use the provisional direction as a hypothesis to test, not as an immutable rule;
3. do not invent missing brand decisions;
4. record meaningful new decisions here only after UXIE accepts them.

## 2. Status vocabulary

- **Confirmed:** explicitly accepted and safe to apply.
- **Provisional:** a useful working direction that must remain easy to change.
- **Open:** not decided yet; explore options before standardising.

## 3. Brand foundation

### Confirmed

- The brand name is always written **UXIE**.
- UXIE should be perceived as a **digital partner**, not as a boutique studio or a
  conventional consultancy.
- “Digital partner” spans design, websites and business optimisation. Technology
  supports those improvements; it is not the proposition by itself.
- The desired personality, in order of priority, is:
  1. human;
  2. premium;
  3. technological;
  4. creative;
  5. strategic.
- UXIE should demonstrate expertise and inspire confidence without looking overly
  technical.
- The language used in an artifact depends on the client and context. The visual
  identity must therefore work in Spanish and English.

### Interpretation

The visual identity should balance competence with approachability. It should feel
considered and contemporary, but not cold, opaque or fascinated with technology
for its own sake.

“Premium” should come primarily from restraint, typography, composition, detail
and execution quality—not from ornamental luxury cues.

## 4. Core design principles

### 4.1 Human clarity

Make information understandable before making it impressive. Prefer plain
language, legible typography, clear hierarchy and interfaces that reveal what the
user can do.

Avoid impersonal corporate language, unexplained technical jargon and visual
devices that make the experience feel machine-made or distant.

### 4.2 Quiet confidence

Use a small number of strong visual decisions. Generous space, disciplined
alignment and precise details should communicate confidence without excess.

Avoid crowded compositions, gratuitous decoration, exaggerated claims and visual
noise added merely to make an artifact appear “designed”.

### 4.3 Technology without the cliché

Technology can appear through structure, interaction, product evidence and subtle
graphic behaviour. It should not depend on predictable AI or technology imagery.

Avoid defaulting to robots, brains, circuit patterns, glowing neon grids, generic
dashboards or dense futuristic interfaces.

### 4.4 Expressive restraint

UXIE may use a distinctive type treatment, an unexpected composition or visible
motion as a focal point. The surrounding design should remain calm enough for that
gesture to have meaning.

### 4.5 Improvement made tangible

UXIE sells improvements rather than technologies. Whenever possible, design
should make the situation, intervention and outcome understandable. Evidence,
process and results should take precedence over lists of tools.

### 4.6 Flexible coherence

The system should adapt across clients, languages and formats without making every
artifact identical. Consistency should come from shared principles, hierarchy,
spacing, typography and behaviour—not from repeating one decorative motif.

## 5. Visual direction

### Confirmed

- The overall direction combines technological, warm, minimal, human and elegant
  qualities.
- Light mode is the primary presentation.
- Layouts should feel spacious and elegant.
- The system remains flexible while the identity is developed.
- No colour is currently mandatory or prohibited.
- Straight-edged geometry is the preferred starting point, but it may be tested
  against subtly rounded alternatives.

### Provisional

- Start with a warm or soft neutral light background rather than clinical pure
  white everywhere.
- Use dark neutral text with strong contrast.
- Explore one vivid accent colour supported by a restrained neutral palette.
- The electric blue seen in the current UXIE website is a candidate for that
  accent, not an accepted token or final colour.
- Prefer strong grids, long alignments, large areas of negative space and a small
  number of deliberately scaled elements.
- Use borders, tonal surfaces or layering sparingly to organise information.
- Shadows, when tested, should be subtle and functional rather than decorative.
- Avoid turning the current website's gradient, sphere and wave motifs into brand
  elements without explicit review.

### Open

- Final logo and wordmark.
- Colour palette and accessible colour tokens.
- Corner-radius language.
- Depth system: flat, bordered, layered or softly shadowed.
- Grid, spacing scale and responsive layout rules.

## 6. Typography

### Confirmed

- Typefaces must be free to use and suitable for web use or self-hosting.
- Body copy should use a neutral, highly legible typeface.
- Display typography may be more technical or expressive.
- The hierarchy must work in both Spanish and English.

### Provisional

- Explore a restrained two-voice system: a neutral sans serif for functional and
  extended reading, paired with a distinctive display face or display treatment.
- Large headlines can provide much of the brand's expression, reducing the need
  for decorative graphics.
- Use few weights and styles. Contrast should come from scale, spacing and role as
  much as from weight.
- Treat uppercase as a label or navigational device, not as the default for long
  headings or paragraphs.

### Open

- Font families and fallbacks.
- Display/body pairing or a single-family system.
- Type scale, line heights, tracking and maximum text widths.

## 7. Image and graphic language

### Confirmed

- Conventional illustration is not part of the desired direction.

### Provisional

Prioritise visual material that provides evidence or creates a controlled
atmosphere, such as:

- thoughtfully art-directed photography;
- real interface or process details;
- diagrams that make systems and improvements understandable;
- cropped product or project views;
- restrained abstract fields, texture or geometry when they support composition.

Avoid generic stock imagery, decorative illustration sets and AI imagery whose
main effect is to announce that it was generated. Generative imagery may be
explored case by case, but it is not a default brand ingredient.

### Open

- Photography art direction.
- Abstract graphic language.
- Use or exclusion of generative imagery.
- Icon family and icon construction rules.

## 8. Motion and interaction

### Confirmed

- Motion should be visible but elegant.
- Accessibility is part of the interaction standard.

### Provisional

- Use motion to establish hierarchy, reveal relationships, provide feedback or
  guide attention.
- Prefer controlled entrances, transitions, hover responses and scroll-linked
  sequences with clear purpose.
- Let one meaningful motion idea lead a section instead of animating every
  element independently.
- Interaction should remain understandable before, during and after animation.
- Always support reduced-motion preferences and preserve full functionality when
  motion is disabled.
- Avoid long blocking intros, motion that delays reading, scroll hijacking and
  effects that exist only to demonstrate technical ability.

### Open

- Motion duration and easing tokens.
- Page-transition model.
- Signature interactions.
- Exact tolerance for scroll-driven effects and pointer treatments.

## 9. Accessibility

### Confirmed

UXIE artifacts should target **WCAG 2.2 Level AA** where the standard applies.

At minimum:

- text and interactive states must meet contrast requirements;
- keyboard access and visible focus must be preserved;
- content structure must remain semantic and understandable;
- information must not depend on colour alone;
- motion must respect reduced-motion preferences;
- text must remain usable when resized;
- documents and presentations should preserve legible type, meaningful reading
  order and sufficient contrast.

Accessibility is a design constraint from the beginning, not a final visual check.

## 10. Application across formats

### Website and digital experiences

Lead with a clear business outcome, strong hierarchy and evidence. Technology may
be shown when it makes the solution more credible or understandable.

### Documents and deliverables

Use consistent cover logic, typography, spacing, colour restraint, page hierarchy
and data presentation. Client-facing content may adapt to the client's language
and context while retaining UXIE's clarity and quality.

### Presentations

Prefer one idea per slide, large readable type and diagrams or evidence with a
clear purpose. Do not compress a document onto slides.

### Future products and interfaces

Apply the same principles, but define functional component and token rules only
when a real product need exists. The current version does not prescribe a UI
component library.

## 11. Reference interpretation

These references indicate qualities to learn from, not styles to copy.

### HappyRobot

Reference: <https://www.happyrobot.ai>

- restrained, spacious composition;
- strong editorial-scale typography;
- limited, warm neutral palette;
- technology communicated through structure and product context rather than
  visual noise.

### Legency Media

Reference: <https://legencymedia.com/>

- interaction used to give a simple composition more character;
- bold accent colour against a light, neutral foundation;
- clear calls to action and modular information blocks.

### Mazette

Reference: <https://www.mazette.co/>

- movement used as part of the brand experience;
- expressive display typography paired with generous whitespace;
- soft atmospheric colour used without overwhelming the content.

### Current UXIE website

Reference: <https://uxiesolutions.com>

The current identity may be reconsidered completely. Its vivid blue is worth
testing as a possible link to the existing brand, but no current logo, colour,
gradient, typeface, shape or layout should be treated as canonical by default.

## 12. Initial do / do not guidance

### Do

- create a calm base and choose a small number of expressive moments;
- communicate outcomes and expertise in language clients can understand;
- use space and typographic hierarchy deliberately;
- make interaction feel polished and purposeful;
- show evidence, process and real work whenever it exists;
- test choices across web, documents and presentations before standardising them.

### Do not

- make UXIE look like a generic AI startup or a conventional corporate consultancy;
- overload layouts with technology clichés, effects or competing accent colours;
- confuse premium with ornamental, exclusive or inaccessible;
- copy a reference site's recognisable visual devices;
- standardise components or tokens before there is enough evidence to choose them;
- present provisional ideas as accepted brand rules.

## 13. Decisions still needed

The next useful design phase should resolve these questions through visual
exploration rather than discussion alone:

1. logo and wordmark direction;
2. final primary and supporting palette;
3. typography candidates and pairing;
4. corner and depth language;
5. photography or abstract art direction;
6. icon style;
7. a small set of signature motion behaviours;
8. core audience and the desired first emotional impression;
9. verbal tone by language and context;
10. the first reusable rules for documents and presentations.

Component definitions, detailed tokens and implementation guidance should follow
after this initial visual direction is tested and accepted.

## 14. Governance

UXIE owns and approves the system. When a design exploration resolves an open
question:

1. record the chosen direction and why it was selected;
2. change its status from open or provisional to confirmed;
3. add exact tokens or rules only when they have been validated in real artifacts;
4. remove superseded guidance rather than leaving contradictory rules;
5. update the version and date at the top of this document.
