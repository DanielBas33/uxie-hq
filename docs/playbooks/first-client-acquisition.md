# First Client Acquisition Playbook

> Purpose: guide UXIE from its current early stage to its first credible client results and case studies.
>
> This playbook is intentionally independent from any specific demo, industry or use case. Proof assets should evolve without requiring the acquisition process to change.

## 1. Objective

Obtain the first real UXIE clients, deliver useful outcomes and turn the work into credible proof.

The initial priority is not maximizing revenue. It is to:

- learn what clients will buy
- validate UXIE's services and positioning
- build real case studies
- improve delivery and commercial judgment
- create a foundation for projects worth at least EUR 1,000

Review and improve this playbook after UXIE has its first client results. Until then, treat it as the default operating process.

## 2. Scope

This playbook covers both UXIE service areas:

- AI and business automation
- digital experiences, UX/UI and modern websites

It applies to Spanish and international prospects. Communication and materials should use the prospect's language rather than imposing one language across every channel.

The initial target range is solo professionals and small businesses that would reasonably hire a small specialist partner. Medium and large organizations are not the current focus.

The initial market should remain a testable hypothesis. UXIE may run focused campaigns around different industries, problems or services without changing this playbook.

## 3. Operating principles

### Lead with the improvement

Explain the business problem and desired outcome before discussing technology.

### Show proof early

Do not wait for a large portfolio. Use the strongest relevant proof currently available:

- a real case study
- a working demo
- a concept project clearly labeled as such
- a short personalized walkthrough
- UXIE's own website and digital experience

Demos are replaceable assets. They live outside this playbook and may be added, changed or retired as UXIE learns.

### Start narrow

The first engagement should solve one clear problem with an MVP-sized scope. Avoid open-ended transformation projects before trust and working practices are established.

### Measure real results

Capture the starting situation before delivery whenever a result can be measured. Do not invent outcomes or present estimates as observed results.

### Learn through small experiments

Do not assume outreach volumes, conversion rates, ideal industries or winning use cases. Test them, record what happens and adjust using UXIE's own evidence.

### Protect delivery capacity

UXIE is currently operated alongside regular jobs. Projects must allow asynchronous delivery and must not require routine availability during normal working hours.

Ongoing support is acceptable when expectations, response times and availability are compatible with this constraint.

## 4. Initial commercial approach

UXIE should mainly pursue fixed-scope projects.

Hourly work is acceptable only when the work is strategically interesting and the schedule is genuinely flexible.

The long-term intention is to avoid projects below EUR 1,000. The first one or two projects may be free or discounted when they offer meaningful strategic value, such as:

- access to a real business problem
- a credible portfolio result
- measurable learning
- permission to publish a case study
- a testimonial if the work succeeds
- useful introductions or access to a relevant market

Free work is not the default offer. It is a temporary portfolio investment and must remain an MVP.

After the first free or discounted projects, raise prices progressively based on scope, evidence and demand rather than keeping introductory pricing indefinitely.

## 5. Readiness before outreach

Do only the preparation necessary to support a credible conversation:

1. Publish a simple, modern UXIE website that makes the offer clear.
2. Prepare at least one relevant proof asset; continue building others while outreach is active.
3. Create a prospect pipeline spreadsheet.
4. Define the scope and constraints of any pilot being offered.
5. Be ready to explain the problem UXIE can improve in plain language.

Do not delay outreach until the complete brand, website or portfolio feels finished.

### Pipeline storage

Keep the live pipeline outside the Git repository, preferably in a simple spreadsheet. It may contain personal and commercially sensitive information that should not be committed to UXIE HQ.

Recommended fields:

- contact
- company
- source or channel
- relevant context
- suspected problem
- service or campaign
- status
- last contact
- next action and date
- outcome or reason lost

UXIE HQ may hold the process and a blank structure, but not live prospect data.

## 6. Choosing a campaign

Before each outreach experiment, choose:

- one type of prospect
- one recognizable problem or desired improvement
- one relevant UXIE service or combination of services
- one proof asset
- one channel
- one simple next step

This is a campaign hypothesis, not a permanent ideal customer profile.

Prefer non-regulated, operationally simple businesses while UXIE develops its delivery experience. Avoid initial work involving highly sensitive data or unnecessary compliance risk.

Current automation exclusions:

- voice automation
- WhatsApp automation

These exclusions can be reconsidered later based on capability and demand.

## 7. Acquisition channels

### Warm introductions

Use existing relationships and new introductions when there is genuine relevance. Ask about the person's real situation rather than sending a generic agency pitch.

The warm network can be developed over time; a limited starting network should not block other channels.

### Targeted cold email

Research the business before contacting it. Use UXIE as the sender identity and keep the message focused on one observed or plausible business problem.

Personalized screen-share videos may be used for selected high-fit prospects when showing the idea is more persuasive than describing it.

### Local businesses

Prioritize businesses where a visible digital weakness or plausible operational problem creates a legitimate reason to start a conversation. Local access and trust are advantages, but do not assume a problem without discovery.

### Upwork

Use Daniel's individual profile, since the marketplace is oriented toward freelancers. Treat Upwork as both an acquisition channel and a source of evidence about current client needs.

Prioritize:

- fixed-price or flexible work
- clearly scoped problems
- clients who value business understanding and reliable delivery
- opportunities where UXIE can show relevant proof

Avoid competing solely on price or accepting work that requires daytime availability.

## 8. Outreach process

1. Research the prospect enough to establish relevance.
2. Identify one likely problem or improvement opportunity.
3. Select the most relevant proof asset.
4. Contact the prospect with a short, personalized message.
5. Offer one easy next step, normally a short conversation or walkthrough.
6. Follow up once after a reasonable interval when there is no response.
7. Record the result and next action in the pipeline.
8. Stop pursuing a prospect when there is no signal of interest after the planned follow-up.

Do not claim to know the prospect's internal problem from external observation. Present it as a hypothesis to validate.

## 9. Qualification and discovery

Before proposing a project, understand:

- what happens today
- who performs or experiences the process
- why the current situation is a problem
- frequency and volume
- time, delay, errors, lost opportunities or other consequences
- systems, content and data involved
- who owns the decision
- constraints and dependencies
- what a useful result would look like
- whether UXIE can deliver it within its availability

Use the conversation to determine whether technology is needed at all. Do not force AI, automation or a rebuild where a simpler improvement would be better.

For work involving UX/UI and implementation, include the relevant UXIE people in discovery according to the knowledge required.

## 10. Project acceptance filters

An initial project should normally have:

- a clear decision-maker
- a narrow, fixed scope
- an asynchronous delivery model
- no requirement for routine availability during normal working hours
- a measurable or demonstrable outcome
- manageable data and compliance risk
- clear client responsibilities and access requirements
- a realistic path to completion

Reject, reshape or defer work when urgency, ambiguity, operational dependency or support expectations make reliable delivery unlikely.

## 11. Pilot agreement

Every project, including free work, requires a short written agreement covering:

- objective and expected outcome
- included scope
- explicit exclusions
- responsibilities and required client input
- timeline and review points
- third-party services and costs
- data access and security considerations
- acceptance criteria
- ownership and handover
- support after the MVP
- case-study and testimonial permissions

Free work should require active client cooperation and permission to document the result. If the client does not provide what is needed to make the engagement strategically useful, reconsider offering it for free.

## 12. Delivery and evidence

### Before building

- record the current situation
- capture relevant baseline evidence
- confirm scope and acceptance criteria
- identify risks and dependencies

### During delivery

- communicate progress and blockers clearly
- keep credentials and sensitive data secure
- test expected flows and failure cases
- prevent unapproved scope expansion
- document what the client will need to operate the result

### After delivery

- validate the result against the agreed criteria
- compare the outcome with the baseline when possible
- record limitations and follow-up opportunities
- complete the handover
- request feedback and a testimonial
- request permission for the final case study
- ask for relevant introductions when the client is satisfied

## 13. Case studies

A real case study should explain:

1. the client and context
2. the initial situation
3. the problem or opportunity
4. UXIE's approach
5. what changed
6. the observed result
7. the client's perspective, when available

Clearly distinguish observed outcomes from projections. Anonymize the client when required, and never imply that a demo or concept project was paid client work.

## 14. Weekly operating rhythm

Initial capacity is approximately one hour per weekday plus two to three hours on each weekend day.

Default allocation:

- two weekday hours: portfolio or proof assets
- two weekday hours: prospect research and outreach
- one weekday hour: follow-ups, pipeline maintenance and review
- weekend: concentrated building, delivery or website work
- calls: scheduled separately when necessary

Adapt the allocation when active client delivery becomes the highest priority.

## 15. Review and learning

Review the pipeline once per week. Track enough information to answer:

- Which prospects and problems generate replies?
- Which proof assets create interest?
- Which channels produce useful conversations?
- Where do prospects stop progressing?
- What objections repeat?
- Which work fits UXIE's capacity and strengths?
- Which projects could lead to stronger proof or relationships?

Begin with small experiments rather than arbitrary activity quotas. Establish targets only after UXIE has enough evidence to choose sensible numbers.

Record durable conclusions in the appropriate UXIE HQ context or decision files. Do not promote an untested hypothesis into the ideal customer profile or permanent service structure.

## 16. Exit condition

This version of the playbook remains active until UXIE has its first credible client results and case studies.

At that point:

1. review actual acquisition and delivery evidence
2. update positioning, offers and pricing where justified
3. decide whether to narrow the ideal customer profile
4. revise this playbook for the next stage
